Employment Standards in Ontario

Ministry of Labour — Fair at Work Ontario

The Employment Standards Act, 2000 (ESA) protects employees and sets minimum standards for most workplaces in Ontario.

Employers are prohibited from penalizing employees in any way for exercising their ESA rights.

What you need to know

Minimum Wage

Most employees are entitled to be paid at least the minimum wage. For current rates visit: Ontario.ca/minimumwage

Hours of Work and Overtime

There are daily and weekly limits on hours of work, and rules around meal breaks, rest periods and overtime.

Public Holidays

Ontario has a number of public holidays each year. Most employees are entitled to take these days off work and be paid public holiday pay.

Vacation Time and Pay

Most employees earn vacation time after every 12 months of work. There are rules around the amount of vacation pay an employee earns.

Termination Notice and Pay

In most cases, employers must give advance written notice when terminating employment and/or termination pay instead of notice.

Leaves of Absence

There are a number of job-protected leaves of absence in Ontario. Examples include pregnancy, parental, family caregiver, personal emergency leave (paid and unpaid) and sexual and/or domestic violence leave (paid and unpaid).

Other Employment Rights, Exemptions and Special Rules

There are other rights, exemptions and special rules not listed on this poster including rights to severance pay and special rules for assignment employees of temporary help agencies.

Learn more about your rights at: Ontario.ca/employmentstandards

Customer Service — "Our Guests are Like God"

"Mahimāna raba kahi rahē hana"  |  "Les invités sont comme dire Dieu"  |  "Los invitados son como dios diciendo"

1. Do It Right From the Start

While food and service quality are incredibly important, it is the experience guests have from the minute they walk in the door to the minute they exit that counts. We should aim to treat every guest as a guest in our own home.

  • Greet your guests the minute they walk in the door.
  • Don't interrupt — listen intently and pay attention to what they want; be respectful at all times.
  • Be thoroughly versed on your services — this is YOUR responsibility. Ask questions and repeat orders to make sure you get it right.
  • Never make guests feel like you want them to leave.

2. Fix Problems Immediately

No matter how hard you try, something is going to go wrong some time or another. Whatever the problem, your goal is to please the customer. It is important that you deal with problems immediately. Even if you don't know the answer, be confident, and tell the guest "Just give me one minute, I will fix this for you." And then call your Manager.

  • Own the mistake — acknowledge that, yes, there is a problem, and let them know you are very sorry.
  • Stay calm, especially if you don't agree with your customer. Maintain eye contact and watch your body language.
  • Always empathize, don't blame and apologize again!

3. Take Care of Special Requests and Special Circumstances

Occasionally, guests will need special attention. This could be a parent who is juggling their kids and needs extra help, a person with disabilities who needs help, an elderly person who needs assistance, etc.

  • Be respectful — at all times — even if their disability is confusing or unbelievable. Do not mock, or belittle the person — and always ask "How can I help?" before offering any assistance.
  • Take care of them, just like they were your family member.

Customer Service Standard Policy (AODA) – 2025
Unsigned ▼

Customer Service Standard Policy

We strive at all times to provide our products in a way that respects the dignity and independence of people with disabilities. We are also committed to giving people with disabilities the same opportunity to access our goods and services.

Communication

Our employees will communicate with people with disabilities in ways that take into account their disability. We will train staff who communicate with customers on how to interact and communicate with people with various types of disabilities.

Use of Service Animals and Support Persons

We welcome people with disabilities who are accompanied by a service animal on parts of our premises that are open to the public. We will also ensure that all staff, volunteers and others dealing with the public are properly trained in how to interact with people with disabilities who are accompanied by a service animal.

Any person with a disability who is accompanied by a support person will be allowed to enter the premises with his or her support person. At no time will a person with a disability who is accompanied by a support person be prevented from having access to his or her support person while on our premises.

Notice of Temporary Disruption

We will provide customers with notice in the event of a planned or unexpected disruption in the facilities or services usually used by people with disabilities. This notice will include information about the reason for the disruption, its anticipated duration, and a description of alternative facilities or services, if available. The notice will be placed at all public entrances and service counters on our premises.

Training for Staff

The Company will provide training to all employees who deal with the public and all those who are involved in the development and approval of customer service policies, practices and procedures. This training will be provided as part of the Choice University courses and will be completed within the first month of employment.

Training will include the following:

  • The purposes of the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2005 and the requirements of the customer service standard
  • How to interact and communicate with people with various types of disabilities
  • How to interact with people with disabilities who use an assistive device or require the assistance of a service animal or a support person
  • How to use equipment or devices available on our premises that may help with the provision of goods or services to people with disabilities
  • What to do if a person with a disability is having difficulty in accessing our services

Applicable staff will be trained on policies, practices and procedures that affect the way goods and services are provided to people with disabilities. Staff will also be trained on an ongoing basis when changes are made to these policies.

Feedback Process

Feedback regarding the way we provide goods and services to people with disabilities can be made via: Email, In-person, Telephone, Letter, or Website.

All feedback will be directed to Upreet Dhanoa (President). Customers can expect to hear back in 2 business days.

Accessible Workplace Policy

DG Hotels are committed to improving accessibility. We will put the following policies into practice as required by the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act.

General

  • DG Hotels is committed to training staff on Ontario's accessibility laws and on accessibility aspects of the Human Rights Code that apply to persons with disabilities.
  • Training will be provided in a way that best suits the duties of employees, volunteers and other staff members.

Information and Communications

  • DG Hotels is committed to meeting the communication needs of people with disabilities.
  • When asked, we will provide information and communications materials in accessible formats or with communication supports.
  • This includes publicly available information about our goods, services and facilities, as well as publicly available emergency information.

Employment

  • DG Hotels will notify the public and staff that, when requested, we will accommodate disabilities during recruitment and assessment processes and when people are hired.
  • If needed, we will provide customized workplace emergency information to employees who have a disability.
  • If using performance management, career development and redeployment processes, we will take into account the accessibility needs of employees with disabilities.

Design of Public Spaces

DG Hotels will meet the Accessibility Standards for the Design of Public Spaces when building or making major modifications to public spaces. Public spaces include:

  • Recreational trails/beach access routes
  • Accessible off street parking
  • Service-related elements like service counters, fixed queuing lines and waiting areas

Modifications to this or Other Policies

  • Any of our policies that do not respect and promote the dignity and independence of people with disabilities will be modified or removed.

Discrimination, Harassment, Bullying and Violence in the Workplace Policy

Employees are expected to treat one another with mutual respect, and must commit to fostering a work atmosphere free of discrimination, harassment, bullying, and violence. Acts of workplace discrimination, harassment, bullying, or violence against or by any employee are prohibited. It is also a violation of this policy for anyone to knowingly make a false complaint of discrimination, harassment, bullying, or violence, or to provide false information about a complaint.

DG Hotels is an equal opportunity employer and commits to ensuring every employee and candidate for employment is free from discrimination within employment or prospective employment.

Definitions

  • Bullying: Any inappropriate conduct or comment by a person towards another worker that the person knew or ought to have known would cause that worker to be humiliated or intimidated. This includes cyberbullying.
  • Discrimination: Treating someone differently based on their personal characteristics: race, ancestry, place of origin, colour, ethnic origin, citizenship, religion, creed, sex, gender identity, age, record of offences, marital status, family status, disability, sexual preference, pregnancy, or other protected ground.
  • Harassment: A course of vexatious comments or conduct against an employee that is known or ought reasonably to be known to be unwelcome. This includes sexual harassment, inappropriate physical conduct, and acts that create an intimidating, offensive, degrading, humiliating, or hostile work environment.
  • Sexual Harassment: Harassment against an employee on the basis of sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, or gender expression.
  • Violence: The use or attempted use of physical force against an employee that could cause physical injury.

Requirements

  • Prohibited Actions include: bullying; workplace violence or harassment; any threat or intimidation; possession of a weapon on company property; assault or other acts of hostility or aggression; dangerous or threatening horseplay; retaliation against anyone who has made a complaint or participated in an investigation.
  • Sanctions: Those who violate this policy are subject to disciplinary and/or corrective action, up to and including termination of employment for cause.
  • Safety measures: Employees are strongly encouraged to: remove themselves from any immediate danger; contact emergency services where appropriate; follow all safety policies; immediately inform management about any safety risks; become familiar with the location of all building exits.

Reporting Complaints and Incidents

An employee who is a victim of harassment, bullying, discrimination, or violence by another employee may inform the wrongdoer that the wrongdoer's comments or conduct is unwelcome. If the employee is not comfortable doing so, the employee must report the problem to his or her manager. If the manager is the source of the problem, the report must be made to the next level of management or the franchisee.

Threats or assaults of a serious nature must be reported to the local police. If an incident causes the employee to fear for his or her safety, the employee must call 9-1-1 immediately.

No Retaliation

There must be no retaliation against anyone who makes a bona fide report of harassment, discrimination, bullying, threats, or violence. Retaliation by another employee is strictly prohibited.

December 2024 – This is intended as a guideline for the franchisee's convenience and not as a required action.

Fitness for Work Policy

Policy Intent

This policy supports the company's commitment to do its part in maintaining a safe and healthy work environment.

Policy Statement

Employees are expected to be fit for work when reporting to their job and to remain fit for the duration of the working day.

Definitions

Fitness for Work: The state in which individuals are in a physical, mental, and emotional condition that enables them to perform their assigned duties effectively and in a manner that does not have a negative impact on themselves or others.

Requirements

Responsibilities of Employees:

  • Employees must not consume alcohol or recreational marijuana on company premises or in company vehicles.
  • They must not be in possession of or use any illicit drugs on company premises, in company vehicles, or at company-sponsored events.
  • If required to take prescription medicine which could impair their ability to safely carry out their work, employees should advise their supervisor.
  • Manage factors that may have an impact on their ability to perform the required work unimpaired.
  • Disclose, to their immediate supervisor or Manager, information about any factors that may impact their ability to perform work safely.
  • Ensure that their judgment and performance at work (including the operation of company vehicles) are never impaired by intoxicating substances, including alcohol and marijuana.
  • If in need of assistance with health challenges that may affect their fitness for work, discuss these concerns with their immediate supervisor and/or Manager.

Enforcement: If there is reason to believe that an employee's job performance is being affected negatively by his or her use of intoxicating substances, the company has the right to inquire as to the nature of the problem and to take appropriate action, which may include disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.

December 2024 – This is intended as a guideline for the franchisee's convenience and not as a required action.

Workplace Health and Safety Policy

We are very concerned and interested in the Health and Safety of employees. Protection of employees from injury or occupational disease is a continuing objective. We will make all reasonable efforts to provide a safe, healthy work environment. Employees and managers share an ongoing responsibility for worker Health and Safety. We strive to reduce the risk of fire and other property damage and to comply with all applicable legislation.

Procedure

  • Management will be held accountable for the Health and Safety of workers under their supervision. Management is responsible to ensure that machinery and equipment are safe and that employees work in compliance with established safe work practices and procedures. Employees must receive adequate training in their specific work tasks to protect their Health and Safety.
  • Management is required to ensure health and safety meetings occur as required by legislation.
  • Every employee must protect his or her own Health and Safety by working in compliance with the law and with safe work practices and procedures established by the company. Employees must report to work mentally and physically fit, rested and free from the effects of drugs and alcohol.
  • Employees must notify managers of any health and safety concerns, so that they may be dealt with promptly. Employees must report any injuries sustained at work that require either first aid or health care to their supervisor immediately.
  • Employees should not report to work if they have an infectious disease, wound, are vomiting or have diarrhea. They should contact management as soon as it becomes apparent that they should not report to work.
  • It is in the best interest of all parties to consider Health and Safety in every activity. Commitment to Health and Safety is an integral part of this hotel.

December 2024 – This is intended as a guideline and not as a required action.
